## 2. Recitals — *Art 34(2)(d)*

**A.** The School has subscribed to EduGear under the EduGear Terms of Service (the "Principal
Agreement"), and any order form or service level agreement executed with it. This DPA forms part
of the Principal Agreement and prevails over it on matters of data protection.

**B.** In providing EduGear, A4 Technologies processes personal data on the School's behalf.

**C.** The School is the **data controller** and A4 Technologies is its **data processor** within
the meaning of the NDP Act.

**D.** The parties enter into this DPA to satisfy section 29(2) of the NDP Act, which requires a
written agreement between a controller and its processor.

---

## 3. Purpose of processing — *Art 34(2)(e)*

A4 Technologies processes personal data solely to provide the EduGear service to the School: the
administration of student records, academic sessions and classes, subjects, results and report
cards, attendance, fee structures, invoicing, payments and receipts, staff accounts and
permissions, the parent portal, and the notifications and audit records that support them.

**A4 Technologies processes personal data only on the School's documented instructions.** The
Principal Agreement, this DPA, and the School's use of the service through its interface together
constitute those instructions. A4 Technologies will not process the data for any purpose of its
own, will not sell or disclose it for value, and **will not use it to train artificial intelligence
models**.

If A4 Technologies believes an instruction breaches the NDP Act, it will say so promptly and may
decline to act on it until the matter is resolved.

### 4.1 Categories of data subject

### 4.2 Categories of personal data

| Group | Data |
| --- | --- |
| Student identity | Name, admission number, gender, date of birth, photograph, email, phone, address, nationality, admission date, status, class placement, free-text notes |
| Academic | Subject scores, assessment components, computed grades and positions, report cards, teaching assignments, attendance registers |
| Financial | Fee assignments and line items, invoices, payments, receipt numbers, payment references, uploaded payment evidence, refund records |
| Guardian | Name, relationship, phone numbers, email, address, occupation |
| Staff and user accounts | Name, email, phone, hashed password, photograph, role and permissions, account status |
| Technical | Sign-in times, failed sign-in attempts, IP address, browser user-agent, audit trail entries, request identifiers |

### 4.3 Sensitive personal data — *section 30*

Where the School chooses to record them, EduGear stores the following, each of which is **sensitive
personal data** under section 65 of the NDP Act:

| Field | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Genotype | Genetic data / health status |
| Blood group | Health status |
| Religion | Religious or similar beliefs |
| State of origin, LGA | Race or ethnic origin |

**These fields are optional and the School decides whether to use them.** The exemption in section
31(4)(b) covers education but does **not** extend to section 30. The School therefore warrants that
it has obtained the **consent of a parent or legal guardian** under sections 30(1)(a) and 31(1)
before recording any of them, and will erase them on request without that affecting any other part
of the record.

**The Processor enforces this in the software rather than relying on the warranty alone.** No value
can be stored in any of these fields unless a consent record exists against that student naming who
gave it, how and when — and section 26(2) is why: the burden of demonstrating consent falls on the
School, and a warranty in an agreement does not discharge it. For records migrated from paper the
School declares the consent once per import file, and that declaration is recorded against every
record it covers. Withdrawal erases the four values and the consent record in a single operation,
after which nothing can be re-entered without fresh consent. Each of these acts is written to the
School's audit trail.

### 4.4 Nature of processing

Collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, retrieval, alteration, use, disclosure
to the School's own authorised users, restriction, erasure and de-identification.

## 6. Location of processing — *Art 34(2)(f)*

Personal data is hosted **outside Nigeria** with the sub-processors listed in **Schedule 3**.

The Nigeria Data Protection Commission has not issued an adequacy decision or a regulatory
instrument on cross-border transfer; Article 45(2) of the GAID directs that Schedule 5 of the GAID
be used in the meantime. A4 Technologies therefore relies on **section 41(1)(a)** — contractual
clauses binding each recipient to a level of protection consistent with the NDP Act — and
maintains the written record of the transfer basis and adequacy assessment that **section 41(2)**
requires. That record is available to the School on request.

Online payments are processed by **Paystack within Nigeria**. Card details are entered with
Paystack directly and never reach EduGear's systems.

A4 Technologies will not transfer personal data to a new country without first notifying the School
and establishing a lawful basis under Part VIII of the NDP Act.

## 7. Obligations of A4 Technologies — *Art 34(2)(a), (i); section 29(1)*

A4 Technologies shall:

**(a)** comply with the principles and obligations of the NDP Act as they apply to the School —
*s.29(1)(a)*;

**(b)** assist the School, by appropriate technical and organisational measures, in honouring data
subject rights under Part VI, including requests for access, correction, erasure, restriction,
objection and portability — *s.29(1)(b)*. Where a request reaches A4 Technologies directly it will
be referred to the School without undue delay and not answered independently;

**(c)** implement and maintain the technical and organisational measures in **Schedule 2** to
secure the data as Part VII requires — *s.29(1)(c)*;

**(d)** provide the School with the information it reasonably requires to demonstrate compliance,
including for the School's own Compliance Audit Returns, and allow audit on reasonable notice no
more than once a year unless a breach or a regulator requires otherwise — *s.29(1)(d)*;

**(e)** notify the School before engaging a new sub-processor, giving it a reasonable opportunity
to object — *s.29(1)(e)*;

**(f)** ensure each person authorised to process the data is bound by confidentiality and has been
trained, as Article 46 of the GAID requires;

**(g)** notify the School of a personal data breach **without undue delay and in any event within
24 hours** of becoming aware of it, with the information in Article 33(5) of the GAID, so the
School can meet its own 72-hour obligation to the Commission under section 40(2);

**(h)** assist the School with any data privacy impact assessment and any consultation with the
Commission; and

**(i)** at the end of the Principal Agreement, deal with the data as clause 11 provides.

The School shall:

**(b)** obtain parental consent for the sensitive fields in clause 4.3 before recording them, and

**(c)** ensure the data it provides is accurate and lawfully obtained;

**(d)** manage its own users' access, remove access promptly when a person leaves, and keep

**(f)** respond to data subject requests as controller, with A4 Technologies' assistance; and

**(g)** **register with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.** Primary and secondary schools
are data controllers of major importance by sector under Schedule 7 ¶1(1)(c)(iii) of the GAID,
irrespective of size, and registration is the School's own legal obligation.

## 10. Confidentiality — *Art 34(2)(n)*

Each party shall keep the other's confidential information, and all personal data processed under
this DPA, in confidence; use it only to perform this DPA; disclose it only to personnel who need it
and who are bound by equivalent obligations; and protect it with no less than reasonable care.

This obligation survives termination indefinitely in respect of personal data, and for three years
in respect of other confidential information. It does not apply to information that is public
through no fault of the recipient, was lawfully known to them beforehand, or must be disclosed by
law — in which case the disclosing party will give notice where it lawfully may.

## 11. Tenure, return and deletion — *Art 34(2)(o)*

This DPA takes effect on the date the Principal Agreement begins and continues for as long as A4
Technologies processes personal data on the School's behalf.

On termination, at the School's election:

- A4 Technologies will make the School's data available for **export in a structured, commonly
  used, machine-readable format** for **90 days**; and
- after that period, or earlier on the School's written instruction, will **delete or irreversibly
  de-identify** it, and confirm in writing that it has done so.

Export is available even where fees are outstanding. Where law requires A4 Technologies to retain
a record, it will retain only that record, only for that reason, and keep it protected by this DPA.

## 12. Specific restrictions — *Art 34(2)(p)*

A4 Technologies shall **not**:

- process personal data for any purpose other than providing the service;
- use personal data to train artificial intelligence or machine learning models;
- sell, rent or disclose personal data for value;
- combine or enrich the School's data with another school's data, or with data from any other
  source, except in irreversibly anonymised form for aggregate service statistics;
- market to students, parents or staff using data obtained through the service;
- grant any employee access beyond what their role requires;
- impersonate or sign in as a School user. **The platform administration console is metadata only
  and provides no such facility**; and
- transfer data to a new jurisdiction or sub-processor without notice under clauses 6 and 7(e).

# Schedule 2 — Technical and organisational measures — *Art 34(2)(j)*

**Tenant isolation.** Each school's data is separated at the database layer by an enforced
isolation rule applied to every query. The rule **fails closed**: a query that cannot establish
which school it belongs to is refused rather than answered. Cross-tenant access is covered by
automated tests that run on every change.

**Authentication.** Passwords are stored as **argon2 hashes** and are not recoverable. Repeated
failed sign-ins lock an account. Sessions are short-lived and are **revoked immediately** on
password change, sign-out, role change, or suspension of the user, their membership or the School.

**Authorisation.** Access is permission-based, not role-name-based. A teacher may enter scores only
for the subjects and classes they are assigned to; a parent may see only their own children. No
user may grant a permission they do not hold, and no user may change their own role.

**Encryption.** All traffic is encrypted in transit using TLS. Data at rest is encrypted by the
hosting and storage providers in Schedule 3.

**Uploads.** Files are validated by **reading their actual bytes**, not by trusting the declared
file type. Size and type are restricted per purpose, filenames are sanitised, and storage keys
contain no caller-supplied input.

**Audit trail.** Every significant change records the actor, the action, the affected record, the
time, and the request identifier. Audit writes cannot be disabled by a user.

**Rate limiting.** Requests are rate-limited per client ahead of authentication, so an
unauthenticated flood cannot exhaust resources.

**Error reporting.** Error reports are rebuilt from an allowlist before leaving our systems and
carry **identifiers only** — request id, school id, user id. Names, email addresses, request bodies
and log text are stripped, and breadcrumbs are dropped entirely.

**Payment data.** Card details are never received or stored. The School's bank account number is
held by Paystack, not by EduGear; EduGear retains only the bank name, the account name returned by
the bank, and the last four digits.

**Change control.** Changes pass automated linting, type checking, unit and integration tests, and
a production-mode boot check before release. Database migrations run as a gated release step.

# Schedule 4 — Risks and mitigations — *Art 34(2)(k), (l)*

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| One school's data exposed to another | Database-level isolation that fails closed; automated cross-tenant tests |
| A parent sees another family's child | Guardian relationship checked on every portal request; a non-ward returns "not found" |
| Staff account compromised | Argon2 hashing, lockout on repeated failures, short sessions, immediate revocation on change |
| Over-broad staff access | Granular permissions; no self-granting; no self-role-change |
| Sensitive student data recorded without consent | Rejected by the Service unless a consent record exists for that student (who, how, when); declared per file on import; withdrawal erases values and record together; every act audited |
| Children's data exposed by error reporting | Identifiers only; names, emails and bodies stripped; breadcrumbs dropped |
| Data outside Nigeria without a lawful basis | Contractual clauses under s.41(1)(a); written transfer record under s.41(2) |
| Payment fraud or tampering | Webhooks authenticated by signature over raw bytes; provider amount checked against the invoice; duplicate deliveries rejected |
| Data retained longer than necessary | Retention schedule; de-identification of residual records |
| Breach not reported in time | 24-hour processor-to-controller notification under clause 7(g) |
